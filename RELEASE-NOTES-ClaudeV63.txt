InvoiceGeneratorNow ClaudeV63

Date entry: native picker on touch, relative due-date presets, and the
document output kept locale-independent.


What was already there
- The app already had two proper native date inputs, g_issue and g_due, both
  type="date", sitting in the Details card. Tapping either has always given
  you the iOS wheel or the Android calendar.
- What you see on the document is a mirror of those values, rendered as an
  editable span bound to the input. So the picker existed; it simply was not
  where you were tapping. This release connects the two rather than building
  anything new.


What best practice actually says
- The guidance across GOV.UK, NHS, NSW and the US CMS design systems splits on
  one question: does the user already know the date, or are they choosing one?
  Text entry wins for dates a user is copying, such as a birthday. A picker
  wins when the date is near the present day and the day of the week matters.
- Invoice dates are the second case. The issue date is nearly always today or
  within a few days, and a due date is a forward date where "is that a
  Saturday" is a real question. A picker is appropriate here.
- Three conditions come with it, and all three are honoured:
    1. Text entry must still work. Desktop keeps direct typing untouched.
    2. The picker is an enhancement, not the mechanism. The underlying field
       is a native input and works on its own.
    3. The displayed format must not be ambiguous.


The format decision, and what was deliberately NOT done
- The obvious change would be to put an <input type="date"> into the document
  itself. That was rejected on purpose.
- A native date input renders in the VIEWER'S browser locale. You would see
  15/08/2026, a US client would see 08/15/2026, a German client 15.08.2026,
  and there is no way to control it. On a document you send to customers, in
  an app covering 250 countries and six document languages, that is a
  payment-terms dispute waiting to happen, not a cosmetic issue.
- The document therefore keeps printing ISO 8601, 2026-08-15, which reads the
  same in every country. The value comes straight from input.value, which is
  always ISO for a date input regardless of what the picker displays.
- A note under the issue date now says so plainly: it prints as YYYY-MM-DD.


Tapping a date on the document
- On touch devices, dates in the document are no longer text you type into.
  They are marked as buttons, and tapping one opens the native picker bound to
  that field. Keyboard users get the same on Enter or Space.
- showPicker() requires a user gesture, which a tap provides, and it is
  wrapped so an older browser falls back to focusing the field in the Details
  card and scrolling it into view.
- Desktop is deliberately unchanged. A keyboard is faster than a calendar, so
  direct typing stays. Every new behaviour is gated behind a coarse-pointer
  check.
- Date targets are 44px on touch. Ten characters of text was previously a very
  small thing to hit accurately.


Due-date presets
- The strongest pattern for invoice due dates is not a calendar at all. Terms
  are relative: on receipt, 7 days, 14 days, 30 days. Hunting for the 14th of
  next month on a calendar is the slow path for something that is really an
  offset.
- Four chips now sit under the due date: On receipt, 7 days, 14 days,
  30 days. The calendar remains for anything else.
- Offsets run from the ISSUE date, not from today, so back-dating an invoice
  still produces correct terms. Verified across month rollover, year rollover
  and a leap year:
      2026-08-15 +30  ->  2026-09-14
      2026-12-20 +30  ->  2027-01-19
      2026-02-27 +7   ->  2026-03-06
      2024-02-27 +7   ->  2024-03-05
- The selected chip highlights, and editing the date by hand clears that
  highlight, since it no longer describes the value.


One fix that would otherwise have bitten
- The document refreshes on the form's input event. A date arriving from a
  native picker does not reliably fire input in every browser; it fires
  change. A change listener for date inputs was added alongside, or picking a
  date would have updated the field and left the document showing the old
  value.


Packaging
